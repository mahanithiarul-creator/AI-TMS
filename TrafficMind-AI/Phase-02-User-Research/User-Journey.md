# TrafficMind AI: User Journey Maps

**Research status:** Working journeys based on Phase 01. Validate through shadowing, incident debriefs, and journey interviews.

## 1. Traffic Police: Managing a Junction Incident

| Stage | Actions and touchpoints | Pain points | Opportunities |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginning | Receives report via radio, control center, or public call. | Vague location; duplicate reports; unknown severity. | Consistent incident context and verified location. |
| Assess | Confirms road conditions and assigns officers. | CCTV may be unavailable; queues already spread. | Shared network and field-status picture. |
| Respond | Travels to site; protects scene; directs traffic and diversion. | Limited staff; conflicting diversions; unsafe road-user behavior. | Timely corridor impact information and coordinated messaging. |
| Stabilize | Clears blockage, coordinates towing/medical/fire support. | Secondary queues and conflicts emerge upstream. | Monitor spillback and flag unsafe upstream conditions. |
| End of journey | Reopens traffic; records incident and lessons. | Manual reporting; weak causal evidence. | Structured operational record and outcome review. |

## 2. Traffic Control Center Operator: Responding to Network Disruption

| Stage | Actions and touchpoints | Pain points | Opportunities |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginning | Monitors normal operations across CCTV, sensor, signal, and radio feeds. | High cognitive load; inconsistent data freshness. | Prioritized, health-aware operational overview. |
| Detect and verify | Reviews alert and video; contacts field staff if necessary. | False positives; slow manual verification. | Explainable alert evidence and confidence cues. |
| Coordinate | Applies approved response, informs police/transit/emergency partners. | Systems and authority boundaries are fragmented. | Shared event status and accountable handoffs. |
| Monitor recovery | Watches queues and adjusts within policy. | Local changes may create downstream effects. | Network-aware impact forecasts and safety guardrails. |
| End of journey | Closes event and records operational outcome. | Hard to compare response effectiveness over time. | Before/after performance and review-ready audit trail. |

## 3. Emergency Driver: Reaching a Critical Incident

| Stage | Actions and touchpoints | Pain points | Opportunities |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginning | Receives dispatch, destination, patient/incident priority. | Address uncertainty and route uncertainty. | Current route context provided through authorized operations. |
| En route | Uses sirens/lights, navigates, communicates with dispatch. | Congestion, blocked junctions, unsafe yielding. | Safe, verified priority coordination and obstruction alerts. |
| Approach | Navigates final turns and dense curb activity. | Large vehicles or ambulances may be unable to maneuver. | Advance identification of access constraints. |
| Arrive | Parks safely, transfers care or begins response. | Arrival route can remain blocked for follow-on units. | Keep a safe access/egress path visible to coordinators. |
| End of journey | Transfers patient/handles scene, becomes available again. | Response-time learning is fragmented. | Review route reliability and recurring blockers. |

## 4. City Administrator: Reviewing Mobility Performance

| Stage | Actions and touchpoints | Pain points | Opportunities |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginning | Receives public concerns, policy commitments, and budget pressure. | Competing goals and anecdotal evidence. | Outcome framing across safety, access, reliability, and environment. |
| Investigate | Requests performance reports and agency input. | Siloed definitions and incompatible data. | Shared definitions, baseline, and data provenance. |
| Decide | Prioritizes interventions, funding, and governance. | Vendor claims; equity and maintenance implications. | Scenario evidence, total-cost context, and stakeholder impact. |
| Communicate | Reports to leadership, agencies, and public. | Hard to explain trade-offs without oversimplifying. | Clear, auditable outcome narratives. |
| End of journey | Reviews results and adjusts policy/program. | Benefits can fade without operations and maintenance. | Ongoing outcome monitoring and ownership. |

## 5. Daily Commuter: Completing a Multimodal Trip

| Stage | Actions and touchpoints | Pain points | Opportunities |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginning | Chooses time and route using memory, maps, and transit information. | Estimates may not capture safety or service disruption. | Accurate public travel conditions and accessible alternatives. |
| Access | Walks/cycles to a stop or station and crosses roads. | Long waits, blocked footpaths, unsafe crossings. | Explicit pedestrian/cyclist safety and access outcomes. |
| Travel | Rides or drives through the network. | Congestion, irregular buses, incidents, stress. | Better reliability and disruption management. |
| Transfer/arrive | Connects to next mode and final destination. | Missed connections and unsafe final approach. | Journey reliability measured end-to-end, not only for vehicles. |
| End of journey | Plans return trip based on the experience. | Trust erodes after repeated surprises. | Consistent service and meaningful feedback channels. |
